# Code of conduct

## Scope

This applies to every space the project runs: the repository, its issues and pull
requests, discussions, any chat or forum set up in its name, and any event where somebody
is representing it. It applies to everyone taking part — contributors, maintainers, and
people asking for help — and it applies to conduct in public spaces when it is directed at
this community.

## Expected

- Argue about the code, the claim and the evidence, and treat the person on the other side
  as someone who is also trying to make the thing work.
- Assume a question is asked in good faith. "How does this work" is not a challenge, and
  "this is confusing" is a report about the software.
- Give a reason with a review comment. A change that is refused is owed an explanation, and
  a claim that the tests pass is owed the command that was run.
- Take feedback about a patch as being about the patch.
- Credit work you build on, and keep somebody's private information private.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment, intimidation, discrimination or demeaning commentary on any basis, including
  age, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, experience level, nationality,
  race, religion, sexual orientation or physical appearance.
- Sexual attention or advances that are not welcome.
- Threats of violence, or language that invites it.
- Insults and personal attacks, including about somebody's competence or their reason for
  being here.
- Publishing somebody's private information without their permission.
- Sustained disruption: re-opening a settled decision to wear people down, spam, or
  bad-faith argument.
- Retaliation against anyone who reports a problem or who gives evidence about one.

## Enforcement

Report a problem to <james@honey-badger.org>. If the report concerns the maintainer, say so
and it will be handled by somebody else, or through GitHub's own
[abuse reporting](https://support.github.com/contact/report-abuse) if there is nobody
independent to ask.

Include what happened, who was involved, when and where, and anything that supports it.
You do not have to have been the target to report something, and you can ask to remain
anonymous — that request is respected, though it can limit what can be established.

Reports are read promptly, investigated confidentially, and answered. What follows a
confirmed report is proportional to it and to whether it is a first time: a private word and
an explanation, a request to change behaviour, temporary removal from the project's spaces,
or a permanent ban for something serious or repeated. The reporter is told the outcome.

## Appeals

An enforcement decision can be appealed by writing to <james@honey-badger.org> with what was
decided and why you believe it was wrong. An appeal is read by a maintainer who was not part
of the original decision where one exists, and the answer is final.
